<p>
  During my exploration of data-driven football analytics, I saw the potential in developing intelligent agents within simulated football environments. This idea was echoed in a competition hosted by Manchester City, 
  which focused on training AI agents in simulated football settings 
  (<a href="https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-football/leaderboard" target="_blank">see competition</a>). 
  Their approach on training agents in synthetic environments like those found in 
  <a href="https://github.com/google-research/football" target="_blank">this open-source simulation</a> was undeniably exciting and advanced.
</p>

<p>
  However, looking at this from the perspective of actual football development, I recognized a few limitations:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>Simulations can't capture the full complexity of real football matches due to imperfect physics and limited tactical representation.</li>
  <li>Agents trained purely in synthetic environments often have limited applicability in real-world coaching or player development.</li>
  <li>Such agents are often optimized more for "gaming" the simulation than mirroring real football player behavior.</li>
</ul>

<p>
  Motivated by these observations, I sought a more grounded approach—one that could make a tangible impact on real-world coaching. 
  I came across the paper <a href="https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.03121" target="_blank">Coordinated Multi-Agent Imitation Learning</a>, which uses actual match position data to imitate real player movement. 
  When I discussed this approach with our football SMEs (Subject Matter Experts), they agreed it showed promising potential.
</p>

<p>
  The core idea is to replicate coaching behavior: helping players improve by analyzing and correcting their movement—something traditionally done in film rooms or training grounds. 
  I implemented an adapted version of the imitation learning algorithm using a curated dataset of real matches. 
  The goal is that, as more data becomes available, the system could actively support coaches in guiding players based on real-world performance insights.
</p>
