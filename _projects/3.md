<p>This <strong>Agile Sprint-based SDLC</strong> is designed to fit my team’s workflow, focusing on task management, feedback loops, and continuous improvement. The cycle consists of the following phases:</p>

<ol>
    <li>
        <strong>Sprint Planning (1-2 days):</strong> We review priorities, break down Epics, and assign tasks for the sprint.
    </li>
    <li>
        <strong>Sprint Progress (1-2 weeks):</strong> Tasks are worked on and tracked in <strong>JIRA</strong>, ensuring ownership and progress.
    </li>
    <li>
        <strong>Sprint Review:</strong> Task owners present completed work, <strong>SME/QA</strong> validate, and meeting minutes are captured.
    </li>
    <li>
        <strong>Sprint Retrospective:</strong> The team discusses blockers and opportunities for improvement in the next sprint.
    </li>
    <li>
        <strong>Sprint Validation (1-2 days):</strong> The sprint is closed in <strong>JIRA</strong>, and a final review with <strong>SME/QA</strong> is conducted.
    </li>
</ol>

<h5 id="sdbp-project">🛠️ <strong>Software Development Best Practices</strong></h5>
<strong></strong>

In my role as a Tech Lead, I have consistently prioritized the implementation of best practices to ensure efficient, maintainable, and collaborative development. Here are the core principles I established and enforced during my tenure:

<ul>
     <li style="margin-bottom: 10px;" id="code-dev-sdbp">
        <strong>Code Dev. Notes:</strong> Write clear comments to make code easy to read and maintain.
        <table border="1" style="width: 100%; border-collapse: collapse; text-align: center; margin-top: 10px;margin-bottom: 10px;">
            <thead>
                <tr>
                <th style="padding: 8px;">No</th>

                </tr>
            </thead>
            <tbody>
                <tr>
                <td style="padding: 8px;">01</td>
                <td style="padding: 8px;"><b>Header Description</b></td>
                <td style="text-align: left; padding: 8px;">
                    Every new script must have a <b>header with a brief description</b> explaining its purpose and why it is needed. 
                    This helps others quickly understand the script's function without digging into the code.
                </td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                <td style="padding: 8px;">02</td>
                <td style="padding: 8px;"><b>Comment on Function</b></td>
                <td style="text-align: left; padding: 8px;">
                    Add a <b>comment at the top of every function</b> you work on, especially if it is complicated or unconventional.
                    Simple explanations about what a function or block of code does are highly recommended to improve readability and maintenance.
                </td>
                </tr>
                <tr>
                <td style="padding: 8px;">03</td>
                <td style="padding: 8px;"><b>Documentation and Flowchart</b></td>
                <td style="text-align: left; padding: 8px;">
                    For every new feature developed, provide <b>proper documentation</b> that explains <b>how to use</b> it and <b>how it works</b> in a nutshell.
                    Preferably include a <b>flowchart</b>. 
                    Upload the documentation to the <b>dedicated Confluence page</b> for easy access and future reference.
                </td>
                </tr>

    <li style="margin-bottom: 10px;" id="git-rules-sdbp">
        <strong>Git Rules:</strong> To have readable and maintainable repository, our team follow this rule while working collaborativly on Git.
        <table border="1" style="width: 100%; border-collapse: collapse; text-align: center;margin-top: 10px;">
        <thead>
            <tr>
            <th style="padding: 8px;">Rule</th>
            <th style="padding: 8px;">Description</th>
            </tr>
        </thead>
        <tbody>
            <tr>
            <td style="padding: 8px;">01</td>
            <td style="text-align: left; padding: 8px;">
                Only work directly on the <b>main branch</b> if you are absolutely sure of what you are doing — preferably use a <b>separate branch</b>. 
                Production is <b>only pulled</b> from the <b>main branch</b>. 
                The main branch should always be <b>stable and up to date</b>!
            </td>
            </tr>
            <tr>
            <td style="padding: 8px;">02</td>
            <td style="text-align: left; padding: 8px;">
                If you are working on a <b>big feature</b> (determined by the tech lead), implement and work on it on a <b>separate branch</b>. 
                Otherwise, you could work directly on the production branch. 
                Always <b>name your branch clearly</b> with the feature name!
            </td>
            </tr>
            <tr>
            <td style="padding: 8px;">03</td>
            <td style="text-align: left; padding: 8px;">
                <b>DO NOT commit</b> changes to the scene if you have <b>NOT changed anything</b>. 
                If you are unsure about changes, <b>ask the team beforehand</b>. 
                Only commit what you have <b>actually worked on</b>!
            </td>
            </tr>
            <tr>
            <td style="padding: 8px;">04</td>
            <td style="text-align: left; padding: 8px;">
                Avoid commit notes like "<b>many fixes</b>" or "<b>changes to various systems</b>". 
                Be <b>specific and descriptive</b> so it's easier to backtrack. 
                Keep your <b>commit messages clear and readable</b>!
            </td>
            </tr>
            <tr>
            <td style="padding: 8px;">05</td>
            <td style="text-align: left; padding: 8px;">
                <b>Merging is only allowed after passing Sprint Review</b>. 
                Merging to the <b>main branch happens after discussion</b> between the developer and maintainer!
            </td>
            </tr>
        </tbody>
        </table>

<h5 id="fd-project">🔍 <strong>Feature Discovery</strong></h5>
<p>Feature discovery can come from various angles, but ultimately, it needs to involve potential users to gather feedback. Here, I will illustrate one approach to validate a feature idea.</p>
<ul>
    <li id="1-fd">
        <strong>Establish a Customer Outreach Method</strong><br>
        Develop a method for approaching potential users, including how we funnel them into our product. Here is how we did:
        <div class="row" style="margin-top: 20px; align-items: center;">
        <!-- Image Column -->
        <div class="col-md-4 text-center">

            <div class="col-md-8">
                <ol>
                <li>
                    First, reach out to interested parties via various channels, followed by sending an official email invitation.
                </li>
                <li>
                    Then, send a Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) to protect intellectual property (IP) depend on the items.
                </li>
                <li>
                    Next, provide access to the product mockup hosted on Figma, with the note that users will need a Google account to interact with the prototype.
                </li>
                <li>
                    Afterward, send a survey aimed at gauging user interest and gathering initial feedback.
                </li>
                <li>
                    In the Call to Action (CTA) section, ask if the recipient is willing to participate as a pilot partner, and inquire if they would recommend the product to a colleague or peer.
                </li>
                <li>
                    Finally, schedule a follow-up meeting to discuss feedback and potential collaboration.
                </li>
                </ol>

                <td style="text-align: left; padding: 8px;">Identify, collect, and define detailed requirements from stakeholders.</td>

            </tr>
            <tr>
                <td style="padding: 8px;">Design</td>

    <li id="2-pm">
        <strong>Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)</strong><br>
        We use the WBS to break down tasks and create a schedule aligned with each project phase. I derived a several step on how to do it:
        <ol>
        <li><b>Method to Break Down Tasks:</b> Start by identifying the key deliverables of the project. Break each deliverable down into smaller, manageable tasks, ensuring that each task has a measurable success criterion that aligns with the overall project goals. This helps to keep the progress measurable and on track.

        <li><b>Creating a Schedule:</b> Once the tasks are organized by phases, create a timeline for each task based on its priority and dependencies. This schedule will help allocate resources efficiently and ensure that tasks are completed in the correct order, leading to smoother execution throughout the project lifecycle.</li>
